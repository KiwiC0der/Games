How it was made

Terraheim is a mechanics port, the safest of the three AI modding approaches: no original code, art or sound ships here, only the rules and numbers, read out of the games and re-expressed in JavaScript. Both games were decompiled locally with ILSpy from copies we own. Terraria is a .NET/XNA executable and Valheim's logic lives in a Unity Mono assembly, so both decompile to readable C#.

Terraria — the player

The vanilla path of Player.Update for an unequipped player on dry ground, in Terraria's exact order: stat reset, fall damage, HorizontalMovement, JumpMovement, gravity, step smoothing, StepDown / StepUp, DryCollision → Collision.TileCollision, SlopingCollision, BordersMovement. It runs at 60 ticks a second in 32-bit floats, like the original.

Valheim — the structure

WearNTear.UpdateSupport: each piece takes the best support it can get from the pieces it touches. A neighbour d metres away passes on support × (1 − loss × (d + 0.1)), with the loss blended between horizontal and vertical by the angle to the contact point. Two contacts on opposite sides (≥ 100° apart) average their support, which is why a bridge held at both ends outlasts a cantilever. Anything touching the ground has full support. Below its minimum a piece takes 100% of its health as damage on the next wear tick. WearNTearUpdater visits every piece about once a second, so collapses ripple outward in one-second waves.

Two quirks are ported on purpose. A new piece starts at full support until its first update. A piece destroyed mid-frame still reports full support to its neighbours until Unity actually removes it at the end of the frame.

Proving it

A test harness copied the relevant methods verbatim out of the decompilation into a C# program, with small stand-ins for the engine types, and ran them side by side with this JavaScript across 280 scenarios: random terrain and inputs, staircases, ceilings, lethal falls, randomly built and demolished structures, and a player walking on bridges as they collapse. Every position, velocity, jump counter, support value and collapse matched bit for bit on all 286,017 frames. Each level also ships with a reference solution that the simulator proves stands within budget.

The bridge between them

One metre is 1.5 Terraria tiles, so a 2 m Valheim beam is 3 tiles long. Pieces are written into Terraria's tile map, so you walk on them with Terraria's real collision. Each frame runs the Terraria tick, then Valheim's wear updater, then Unity's end-of-frame destruction. Your build reach is Valheim's 5 m.
